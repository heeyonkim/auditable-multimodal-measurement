# Research opportunities enabled by the measurement system

The prototype is not a claim that one audio or video feature is intrinsically important. Its larger contribution is to make previously inaccessible behavioral evidence available for theory-driven research. The examples below show how researchers could move from an observable recipe to a substantive design while keeping constructs, rival explanations, and outcomes separate.

| Research opportunity | Observable measure enabled by recordings | Illustrative outcome or comparison | Credible design direction | Critical boundary |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Behavioral adaptation under scrutiny | Within-person deviation in timing, vocal modulation, hesitation, and answer structure relative to a rolling personal baseline | Analyst follow-up, forecast revision, market uncertainty, or subsequent disclosure correction | Executive fixed effects; within-call prepared-versus-Q&A comparison; event-time discontinuities | Deviation is not deception, anxiety, or low confidence |
| Conversational access and hierarchy | Who receives the floor, response latency, turn length, interruption opportunities, and participation concentration | Speaking-up, idea uptake, decision quality, employee retention, or status attainment | Repeated team meetings; person and team fixed effects; policy or leadership changes | Floor allocation does not by itself reveal inclusion or psychological safety |
| Delivery–text divergence | Unusual vocal or temporal delivery when transcript language remains routine | Analyst follow-up, disagreement, forecast dispersion, or audience recall | Compare transcript-only predictions with incremental preregistered audio measures | Divergence is a descriptive mismatch, not concealed intent |
| Strategic consistency across venues | Same leader's behavioral profile across earnings calls, media interviews, hearings, and internal events | Stakeholder-specific adaptation, reputation, perceived authenticity, or message diffusion | Cross-setting matched-person panels with recording-quality controls | Venue and production systems are alternative explanations, not nuisance afterthoughts |
| Editorial responsiveness | Camera cuts, framing changes, and shot duration around conversational transitions or substantive moments | Clip selection, viewer attention, social diffusion, or later media coverage | Compare programs, production formats, live versus edited feeds, and within-video null timing | The measure describes the produced artifact, not the editor's intention |
| Crisis and organizational change | Shifts from a leader's own longitudinal baseline before, during, and after consequential events | Recovery speed, stakeholder response, employee sentiment, or strategic change | Staggered event studies with matched unaffected speakers and pretrend checks | Recording conditions and speech genre must be modeled explicitly |
| Collective leadership and role differentiation | Distribution of speaking time, turn-taking, question routing, and multimodal complementarity across executives | Succession, team stability, analyst access, or strategic breadth | Top-management-team panels and leadership-transition comparisons | Speaker identity and functional role require external validation |
| Context-sensitive construct testing | Competing recipes that encode different theoretical expectations for the same observable evidence | Measurement invariance, predictive transport, or expert agreement across settings | Preregister rival recipes; test invariance and out-of-context degradation | Do not ask a model to impersonate a nationality or treat cultural stereotypes as ground truth |

## Three especially persuasive demonstrations

1. **A variable a transcript cannot contain:** estimate within-person delivery deviation while holding language constant, then show its distribution and reliability across repeated calls.
2. **A relation that exists between modalities:** identify moments when routine words arrive with unusual timing or vocal delivery, or when editorial cuts respond to changes in conversational floor.
3. **A reusable research workflow:** use the measure-specification prototype to define rival measures, attach validation evidence, export versioned recipes, and preserve negative tests.

## How outcomes should enter the proposal

Outcomes establish scientific relevance, but the proposal need not produce a publishable causal result. The strongest Stage 1 use is a worked bridge: demonstrate that a new observable varies at scale, connect it to one proximal outcome, retain robustness and null results, and show how future researchers could attach theoretically appropriate outcomes. This avoids turning a general measurement infrastructure proposal into a single underpowered substantive paper.

## Recommended Stage 2 expansion

Scale the earnings-call panel across more executives and firms; preserve repeated observations per person; preregister one or two recipes before examining outcomes; validate selected clips with trained coders; and estimate reliability, convergent validity, discriminant validity, and incremental value beyond text. For video, expand across programs and production formats before interpreting camera behavior or visible action as an individual attribute.
